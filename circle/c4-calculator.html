How it works. Reserve income is USDC in circulation times the yield on the reserve. Partners are paid a part that rises with circulation and a part that rises with reserve income. Circle's quarterly filing says a one-point change in rates moves reserve income by $739 million and distribution costs by $371 million with circulation held at $73.9 billion, so the calculator passes 50.2 percent of each extra dollar of interest to partners, and sets the rest of the payment so that the third quarter comes out as reported. The third slider scales the whole payment up or down. It is labeled with the share partners would take at the third quarter's yield of 3.59 percent.

What is held fixed. Other revenue stays at $102 million a year and running costs at $555 million a year, both the third quarter of 2026 times four. Running costs are Circle's adjusted operating expenses, which leave out stock-based pay, depreciation and restructuring. Holding them fixed flatters the cases where USDC grows a great deal. Token sales such as the ARC presale are left out.

The presets. The two sets of assumptions are the author's, chosen to show how far apart the two cases are. They are not forecasts by Circle or by anyone else. The angel's set uses two years of the 40 percent annual growth in Circle's guidance. The market value is 256.2 million shares at the closing price of $64.98 on November 10, 2026.
